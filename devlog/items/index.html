Dusting off Diablo's loot

How we repainted all 179 of Diablo's inventory pictures, and why the hardest part was leaving them alone.

5 min read9 November 2026

If you played Diablo back then, you know this grid by heart: ten slots across, four down, and the red tint saying you're too weak for what you just found. We spent whole evenings at Griswold's counting gold we didn't have.

A splint mail, then and now. Drag the divider:

All 179 pictures got this, the eleven mouse cursors in their file included, each checked against 1996 at every step. Here's how: an upscaler that wasn't enough, five painting models in a bake-off, the game's own colours and a little shine.

Bigger is the easy part

Brutal Diablo draws the whole game four times wider and taller than the original 640×480. The inventory hadn't even tried yet: a sword 28 pixels wide was stretched over 112, and it showed.

For the rest of the game we picked 4x-SGI as the default, until something better turns up. It's an upscaler trained on 1990s computer graphics and made for pre-rendered sprites like Diablo's. It draws each picture four times larger, then we snap the colours back to the game's and cut the outline clean. Below, "4x-SGI" means that whole chain. On the full helm:

The 1996 picture, every pixel four times larger, as Brutal Diablo showed it until now.

Up close you see the limits: flat areas go soft and painted detail smears. Items sit still right under your nose, so for them it wasn't going to do.

We tried to squeeze more out of it: depth models for shape, Potrace to trace the outline and then every colour edge, the upscaler twice, the old pixel-art filters xBR and hqx. Two of them on the same helm, beside 4x-SGI:

Each changed something; none made the helm look painted. The detail wasn't in the picture, so something had to paint it.

Five painters, one bake-off

That takes a painting model. Most are diffusion models: they learned to clean noise out of pictures, step by step, toward a text description. Hand one our picture under a layer of noise and it repaints it as much as we let it, and will just as happily redesign it.

We tried five on 15 items: SeedVR2, Clarity, Nano Banana Pro, SDXL with a tile ControlNet (an add-on network that keeps the painter to our picture's shapes), and FLUX.1-dev with Jasper's upscaler ControlNet. On our items SeedVR2 added very little, and SDXL went plastic the moment it got more freedom. The final three, on 30 items:

Nano Banana Pro painted the prettiest pictures, but it invented too much:

Clarity stayed faithful only at a light touch. Flux won, keeping Blizzard North's shapes from one short description per picture ("butcher's cleaver", "steel gauntlet hand pointer") and the same seed every time, so a rerun paints the same picture.

How much Flux may repaint is one number: 0 changes nothing, 1 starts from pure noise. We stepped it from 0.25 to 0.45 on all 30 items. Watch the blood on the cleaver:

Item
1996.25.30.35.40.45
Strength: 0.40, kept

Up to 0.40 every item gains brushwork and stays itself; at 0.45 the blood grows new shapes. We kept 0.40 and ran the other 149, about half an hour on one rented GPU.

Last, the colours. Flux paints in millions; Diablo shows 256 at a time, and items use only numbers 128 to 254 of those, plus black: the half that never changes between levels, so a sword looks the same in town as in Hell. We snap each pixel to the nearest shade among the runs its 1996 neighbourhood used, which keeps a blue gem blue. A cleaver patch, enlarged three times:

The game's colour tricks need that. An item you can't use is drawn red through a table that swaps each colour (like the monsters' in our blood post); on the game's colours it works as in 1996:

We tried to make them evil

Once the pictures were faithful, we wanted them a bit more ours. This is Brutal Diablo, after all. On an early version we tried grim, blood in the grooves, a hellfire glow, and all three:

Item

The picture every grade started from.

Blood and fire went first: they add what the items never had. Nano Banana Pro's three evil repaints didn't stay either. Grim came closest, two softer takes to the end, and then we shipped none. Brutal Diablo adds detail and gore; the items' colours stay Blizzard North's.

Shape and shine

Faithful, but flat: Flux's steel and leather catch the light alike. Blizzard North rendered Diablo's heroes and monsters from 3D models, and the items look like they got the same treatment. We wanted that shine back.

For the shape, Depth Anything V2 guesses from one picture how far away each pixel is, and from that we work out which way each bit of surface faces. Arkaine's Valor (lighter is nearer):

For the shine, each item's colour runs are marked steel, silver, gold, glass, gems, paint, wood, leather or paper. Gold gets a warm highlight, leather almost none, then the same snap to the game's colours. How strong? A dial:

Item
1996Unlit0.20.41.0
Shading: 0.4, kept

At 1.0 everything looks wet, like somebody licked it, and our first try ran at about that. It also taught us to put a floor under the darkening: without one, a book's pages went black and the snap to the game's colours frayed them pink. We kept 0.4.

One more fix: our first version lit the items from the upper left. We measured the shading in the 1996 pictures, and Blizzard North lit most of them from the upper right, so we flipped it.

Press H

The pictures show up in five places: bag and belt, your cursor, and three that DevilutionX added: the stash, Griswold's visual store and his list with pictures. We make them once and they go everywhere; underneath, the game still thinks in the old sizes and slots. Pick a place, then press H (or the key below) for 1996:

Seventy-one of them are on the items page, beside 1996. Tell us which one we got wrong (there's always one) on our Discord. Brutal Diablo is free and coming early 2027.

Fresh eyes

Looking at the same pictures every day, you stop seeing them. That happened to us here, and it's a big part of why we share the work while it's still in progress.

Right after the items went out, some fans in comment sections told us the blood on the Butcher's Cleaver looked like plastic. They were right, and it took fresh eyes to see it. So we did another round, and for a few of the materials we found a better way to bring them closer to 1996.

We like working this way. We show things early, we listen, and we fix them quickly. If something looks off to you, tell us in the comments or on our Discord. We read every comment, and yours just made the game better.
