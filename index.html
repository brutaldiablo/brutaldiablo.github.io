Why we’re doing it

Did you know that Diablo was first planned as a turn-based game? It never came out that way, so most players never knew. Then one Friday afternoon David Brevik switched it to real time, took the mouse and clicked on a skeleton.

“The Warrior walked over and smacked the skeleton down. And I was like, oh my God. That was awesome.”

David Brevik, Diablo: A Classic Game Postmortem, GDC 2016

We all had our own first skeleton in 1996. With Brutal Diablo, we’re trying to recreate that feeling again.

What it is

Diablo (1996), modded. Same game, same quests, same Butcher, with a visual uplift.

Same game

Nothing rebalanced, nothing “reimagined”. The quests, the monsters, the loot: all where you left them in 1996. The only thing added is running in town. Underneath, it’s still the original game, running as it always did. Brutal Diablo only changes what you see. Miss the old pixels? Press H to switch, anytime, like in Diablo II: Resurrected.

Improved graphics, darker

The game’s art, recreated to match the original. Nothing invented: the same shapes and the same frames, the way your memory says they looked.

Torches that actually light things. Shadows the town never had. Tristram at night is a different place now.

Bloodier

Every monster has several ways to die. The blood sprays, pools on the floor, runs down the walls and stays.

FAQ

The questions that keep coming up under our videos.

Is it out yet?

Not yet. It’s coming in early 2027. Leave your email and we’ll send you one message on the day, nothing else. Or follow us on any of the socials and you’ll hear it there.

How much will it cost?

Nothing. It’s free and it stays free. If you want to help and support the development, you can donate, but a donation buys you nothing extra. No early access, no bonus build, just our eternal gratitude and the fact that you helped make the project come true.

Do I need the original game?

Yes. Brutal Diablo runs on your own copy of Diablo (1996), the old CD or the one sold on GOG. It needs the game’s DIABDAT.MPQ file, the same way DevilutionX does. Once it’s finished, we’ll try to get it listed among the official mods on GOG.

Does it change the gameplay?

No. Brutal Diablo is built on DevilutionX, the open-source port of Diablo, and doesn’t change the gameplay any further. Running in town comes from DevilutionX too. Everything we add is visual.

Is it a remaster?

Yes, as a mod. It’s the game you played in 1996, every picture recreated to match the original, darker and bloodier, and the gameplay left alone. It runs on your own copy of Diablo, and it isn’t Blizzard’s. Press H and the old pictures are back.

Can I play it with the old graphics?

Absolutely. Press H, anytime, just like in Diablo II: Resurrected. Press it again to come back.

Can I turn the gore off?

No. It’s in the name. The same goes for the graphics and everything else: it’s one package, take it or leave it. The only switch is H, back to Diablo (1996). Modders, the devlog is for you: it shows how we make it.

Is there multiplayer?

Yes. Whatever runs on DevilutionX runs here too, multiplayer included: up to four players, as long as everyone has the same version of the mod.

Windows, Mac, Linux? Phones?

PC first: Windows, Mac and Linux. Phones and consoles maybe one day, once the PC version is right.

What about Hellfire?

Not for now, the base game comes first. We loved Hellfire too, so we’ll see.

Is it 3D now?

No. It’s still the 2D game from 1996, drawn in more detail. Some new things, like the deaths we’re working on, start from 3D models of the monsters, but they end up as 2D frames like everything else.

Is this AI slop?

No. But it isn’t all handmade either.

Everything we do has to sit on top of the original game, so we can’t just make things up. Blizzard North made Diablo’s art from 3D models and high-resolution artwork, then shrank it down to fit a 1996 screen, and the originals got lost in the sands of time. What we do is closer to tech archaeology: we try anything that might bring them back believably. A lot of maths, computer graphics tricks, state-of-the-art research, pretrained models for different parts of the process. And yes, AI helps.

Every part of the game goes through hundreds of tries before we find what works for it. There’s no silver bullet. So if you picture us running one prompt overnight on a rented GPU, posting the result in the morning and calling it a day, you couldn’t be further from the truth.

We put our own time, money and sleepless nights into this, and we do it for ourselves. We’re chasing our fever dream. Not yours, ours. We just hope we both share the same one.

The game will carry our signature, on top of everything the original creators made. We’re standing on the shoulders of giants, and it’s an honour. We’d still love to share the process and the result with you, and if you like it, it’s our pleasure.

TL;DR: yes, we use AI in development. So does the rest of the world, Linus Torvalds included. Wake up and welcome to 2026, Wanderer.

Will Blizzard shut it down?

We hope not. It’s a free fan mod, made out of love for their game. It runs only on your own copy of Diablo, and nothing here is for sale.

Who’s making it?

A few Diablo fans from Czechia 🇨🇿, after our day jobs and around our families. Being Czech, a fair amount of beer went into it too 🍻
